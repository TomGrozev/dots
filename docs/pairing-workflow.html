The Human line

One line per stop, directly under **Mode:**:

**Human:** before: <the one thing to review>. Why: <the specific risk>.
**Human:** after: <what to run or show>. Why: <the specific risk>.

A valid Why names one of these

  • A choice that is hard to undo: a module boundary, stored data, a public contract.
  • Taste that tests cannot judge: UI, names the user sees.
  • I asked to oversee it in the quiz.

Not a reason

  • "It creates new files", alone.
  • "Is this the right place", alone.
  • Unsure? The agent recommends afk and says so.
  • Deploys, migrations on real data, sent messages: no Human line. The ask-first rule covers them.

The quiz

  • A numbered list: title, blocked by, what it delivers, mode, Human lines.
  • The hitl tickets in one table: ticket, Human line, Why.
  • The quiz settles before: decisions when it can. The answer goes into the spec, and that Human line goes away.
  • I override any mode. It iterates until I approve.

The two stops

Before stop

  • Shape review: the skeleton is written, then I see the intent, 2 to 3 examples, the placement map (NEW, CHANGED, REUSED, REMOVED, STORED, with a "Why new" line), two checks, and the decisions.
  • Decision only: one question, 2 or 3 options, one reason each.
  • The agent's pick stays hidden until I reply.
  • The build waits until I approve or change the shape and choose in each decision.
  • Later tickets that build on it get edited to match.

After stop

  • The running result, not a plan: a screenshot, a short recording, or real command output.
  • One line on what to react to.
  • The agent fixes real objections and shows again.

Take the pen, any mode: a build worker fails twice on the same problem with no new hypothesis. I get the repro and the ruled-out hypotheses. I take the pen or give a steer.

Diagrams and pages

  • Console: a mermaid block, about 10 boxes at most, short labels.
  • An HTML page on r3 only when I must see the change to judge it:
    • a UI change, with its states;
    • a change that crosses a boundary: a new module or service, or a new data or API shape;
    • one idea larger than about 400 changed lines or 10 files.
  • The designer worker draws it: one diagram, about 9 boxes at most, readable without the text.

Ask vs do

The agent asks me first for:

  1. publishing: commit, push, tag, PR, merge;
  2. deleting or overwriting work it did not create, or rewriting git history;
  3. changing persisted data, a public contract, or an external system;
  4. anything with secrets or credentials.

Everything else: it decides, states the assumption, and records a note.
